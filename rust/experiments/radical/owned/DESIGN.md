# v5: frequency and posting under one key owner

This independent crate implements the owner route described in [POSTING_OWNER_DESIGN.md](../../../POSTING_OWNER_DESIGN.md). It keeps v4's exact, conservative token-disjoint batch certificate and AA run-parity selection. Each pair key belongs to exactly one of W owners. That owner stores its scalar frequency, lazy candidate heap, and one `Vec<u32>` of historical posting positions. Non-AA positions may be unordered. An AA posting is sorted only after selection and before run-parity planning.

For each batch, the coordinator checks the current candidate head of every owner, skips stale heap entries, and selects the same ordered safe prefix as v4. Selected postings move out of owner maps into batch rules without copying their payload. Flat tasks hold a rule rank and a relative slice offset. Exactly W Rayon jobs claim tasks through an atomic cursor. Each job owns W route buckets, so bucket headers scale as W² rather than task count times W. A bucket holds a per-key scalar delta map and `u32` birth positions. Planning determines each birth's final key on the stable corpus snapshot solely to choose its destination; it never stores `(key,pos)` records. Job results retain valid starts by task number. The coordinator moves these vectors into task order in O(number of tasks) metadata work, then applies all mergers in parallel.

Once every apply job has joined, owners aggregate their routed deltas in parallel. Only after full aggregation can they filter by minimum frequency. Owners then decode each birth key from the final token at `pos` and its right neighbor at `pos + length[token]`, append to their unique eligible posting, and validate that the decoded key maps back to the planned owner. This later decode is necessary for adjacent different-rule matches and AA runs: a planned edge may have two freshly assigned IDs only after both endpoint writes finish. The apply→decode barrier is intentional. Initial indexing uses the same position-only routing: physical chunks are scanned in parallel, then owners decode their routed positions and sum weights before threshold filtering.

The first version uses growable `Vec<u32>` postings. Its final persistent position count and allocated capacity are separate metrics; they are not peak counts and exclude selected lists that lived earlier in the call. `Entry` and `HashMap` capacity add further memory. Selected lists remain allocated through planning, where route birth positions coexist with them. Once every planning job joins, the selected lists are dropped before apply; AA retains its chosen starts in Plan chunks and non-AA in task-indexed valid vectors. No full corpus or full index is copied per worker. Historical stale positions remain in an eligible key's list until that key is selected or drops below threshold. The CLI reports `owned_posting_len`, `owned_posting_capacity`, `owner_entry_count`, peak route birth length/capacity, `train_vm_hwm_mib`, and full-process `vm_hwm_mib`. `stored_born_postings` counts only positions appended to eligible owner entries after frequency filtering; `generated_birth_records` includes filtered births.

The coordinator's heap scan remains a possible bottleneck: it inspects up to W heads for each chosen rule and may pop stale candidates. `--heap-policy lazy` is the default. In that mode an old key's frequency decrease does not push another heap entry; when its outdated entry reaches a heap head, the coordinator pops it and reinserts the current eligible frequency. `--heap-policy eager` pushes a new entry on every old-key decrease, leaving old entries to be discarded when reached. Both policies follow the same exact candidate order. Lazy may still retain dead-key heap entries until they reach the head, so its heap length is not the number of live keys. `select_seconds`, `heap_pops`, `heap_refreshes`, `heap_reinsertions`, and the per-batch peak sum of owner heap lengths/capacities expose this cost without rescanning all entries. Other wall-time phases include initial routing and owner construction, AA sort, planning, apply, owner frequency reduction, and birth decoding. The old v4 arena/sort fields remain in the JSON schema for comparison but have no v5 work attached. This prototype does not implement owner candidate pages, route/apply overlap, or compact Box postings; results should be compared using the same fixture, rule cap, worker count, and total CPU budget.
