# Direct decrements for old pairs

`owned_direct` copies the frozen `owned_grouped_inline` kernel. Its same-binary control is `--reduce-mode combined`; `--reduce-mode direct-old` bypasses the temporary combined-delta map for old pairs. Planning, exact batch certification, AA parity, grouped birth chains, inline postings, corpus writes, and owner assignment stay the same.

At a batch boundary, every existing pair contains IDs below `fresh_start`. Every born edge contains at least one newly allocated ID. Therefore an old pair only loses frequency in this batch. Its unique owner can subtract each producer's counted decrement directly. Once the remaining frequency is below the minimum, removing the entry is safe: later decrements cannot make it eligible again. Missing old entries and the already-selected entries need no update. Each applied decrement uses checked subtraction. The proof assumes the planner's valid edge deltas; after early removal we intentionally do not reconstruct an ineligible pair's residual frequency.

Fresh pairs still aggregate checked weighted frequency and physical occurrence count in a temporary hash map. Only after their complete sum is known do eligible pairs receive a posting reservation and heap entry. Ineligible fresh keys do not acquire a permanent owner entry or position allocation. This avoids an additional pending-count field or a persistent map containing every transient key.

With a lazy heap, old decrements publish nothing until head refresh, as in the control. With an eager heap, a temporary set records touched old keys; after all producer fragments finish, each surviving old key publishes exactly one final candidate. Pushing per fragment would increase heap work and make the comparison misleading.

Both modes have expected linear work in routed fragments and no new corpus/index copy. Direct updates remove old keys from the temporary map but can visit the larger owner map up to W times per key instead of once. That cache-locality tradeoff is the experiment; speed is not assumed. The eager policy additionally pays for its touched set. Fill remains a separate joined owner stage.

`old_route_updates` counts routed old-key fragments in both modes. `old_owner_hits` counts successful old-entry decreases, so the direct mode can report several hits per key. `peak_reduce_temporary_keys_sum` and `peak_reduce_temporary_capacity_sum` are the maximum across batches of the sum of owner-local aggregation map sizes/capacities. They bound simultaneous payload capacity; they are not allocated-byte or RSS measurements. The eager touched-set capacity is reported separately. `call_cpu_seconds` follows the native process-CPU clock convention, around the full checked training call; CPU/wall is average occupied CPU cores, including waits that consume CPU.

Validation compares complete rules, frequencies and final tokens across both modes, lazy/eager policies, W1/W2/W4, small and huge chunks, weighted inputs, ties, adjacent matches, AA runs and lengths beyond 255. A focused reducer test crosses the frequency threshold between producer fragments, combines individually ineligible fresh fragments into an eligible pair, and checks one eager publication per surviving old key.
