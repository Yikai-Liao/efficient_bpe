# Parallel BPE training: implementation evidence

This note separates observed source behavior from deductions for our trainer. Source snapshot: [YouTokenToMe commit `f4162d846057a3118222ca04a01b84297eb8a8db`](https://github.com/VKCOM/YouTokenToMe/tree/f4162d846057a3118222ca04a01b84297eb8a8db), inspected locally on 2026-09-29. The [project README](https://github.com/VKCOM/YouTokenToMe/blob/f4162d846057a3118222ca04a01b84297eb8a8db/README.md) advertises multithreaded BPE training. The following describes what its code actually parallelizes; it is not an equivalence or speed claim for our Rust corpus format.

## At most two in-flight rules: a conditional certificate

The task ring has two slots, and main permits `used_ids - finished_cur < 2` ([L910–L915, L1121–L1128](https://github.com/VKCOM/YouTokenToMe/blob/f4162d846057a3118222ca04a01b84297eb8a8db/youtokentome/cpp/bpe.cpp#L1121-L1128)). While one rule is unfinished, main rejects the next candidate if it touches that rule's right token on the left or left token on the right, and also rejects pipelining after a self-pair ([L145–L147, L1153–L1169](https://github.com/VKCOM/YouTokenToMe/blob/f4162d846057a3118222ca04a01b84297eb8a8db/youtokentome/cpp/bpe.cpp#L1153-L1169)). The high queue preserves an intersecting candidate's old count as an upper bound instead of refreshing it during the unfinished rule ([L227–L244](https://github.com/VKCOM/YouTokenToMe/blob/f4162d846057a3118222ca04a01b84297eb8a8db/youtokentome/cpp/bpe.cpp#L227-L244)). Main waits for full worker reports before introducing the first rule's new keys ([L1236–L1271](https://github.com/VKCOM/YouTokenToMe/blob/f4162d846057a3118222ca04a01b84297eb8a8db/youtokentome/cpp/bpe.cpp#L1236-L1271)).

**Inference from that code, not an author-stated proof:** for a non-self merge `xy→z`, every newly created left edge `(L,z)` with `L≠z` comes from an old `(L,x)` edge; every right edge `(z,R)` with `R≠z` comes from an old `(y,R)` edge. Adjacent selected matches `xyxy→zz` create `(z,z)` from the intervening old `(y,x)` edge. Each final new edge has a distinct old boundary, so its weighted frequency cannot exceed that ancestor pair's pre-merge count. These old counts remain queued as possibly stale *upper bounds*. If the best independent contender beats all affected old-pair upper bounds, unseen new pairs cannot beat it. The source tie rule prefers older IDs to a fresh `z` at equal frequency, which matters here. Our packed-key lexicographic tie also prefers each of `(L,x)`, `(y,R)`, and `(y,x)` to its respective new pair because `z` is a fresh, larger ID. This suggests an exact selection certificate for non-self rules, provided all ancestor upper bounds remain represented and no old count is understated. Self-pairs need a separate bound (their selected pair may itself be an ancestor) or a serial fallback. Certifying the next *choice* does not by itself make concurrent corpus updates safe: the YTTM workers still process published rules in order on each owned word. A complete transplant must prove both scoring and update-order safety. The source does not make more than two rules in flight.

## What does and does not transfer

1. A **local-count + candidate-certificate** design can leave old frequencies at their data owners and fetch exact global totals only for contenders. Combined with owner-supplied new-key summaries, this can remove the per-occurrence coordinator reducer. It still has a sequential global rule choice and may require several candidate checks when upper bounds are loose.
2. Whole-word ownership and RLE avoid cross-worker writes and compress repetitive data. They require existing split boundaries and change the training population/scoring here. They do not directly solve one continuous no-pretokenization sequence with balanced workers or preserve our overlapping self-pair ranking.
3. Neither [BatchBPE's author repository](https://github.com/alexandermorgan/BatchBPE) nor its [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.04653) establishes an identical ordered merge trace: the author explicitly describes equality of the resulting byte-string vocabulary despite a different merge order. [BlockBPE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.11941) parallelizes **application of a trained merge table at inference**, not construction of the frequency-ranked table. These are useful adjacent ideas, not evidence of an exact parallel trainer for our contract.

No claim here that YouTokenToMe's README complexity bound or training speed transfers to our representation. Its word deduplication, Unicode/whitespace pipeline, self-pair score, tie order, queue, and two-slot schedule differ materially.
